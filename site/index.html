TWO CLOCKS, ONE COMPARISON

What do ToE and RET mean?

ToE

When does the change become detectable?

Time of emergence is the start of the first ten-year window in which historically large inundations become sufficiently frequent to pass the detection criteria. It is calculated separately with and without levees.

RET

When is the historical record exceeded?

Record-exceedance time is the start of the first ten-year window in which at least two models exceed the historical no-levee maximum under protection. The same RET is used in both comparisons.

Lead time = RET − ToEPositive: detectable signal firstZero: the same window startNegative: record exceedance first

How to read the findings

These are decadal statistical signals, not operational flood forecasts. “Not detected by 2100” does not mean no flood risk. Lead-time curves include only basins where both ToEs and the shared RET are detected; the full-domain classifications also retain the other basins.

Exact-cell queries always use the original grid. Depths ≤0.1 m are transparent on the map but remain queryable. Data & definitions

The basin results use the fixed 426-sub-basin complete-case domain: all 11 models, both protection states, area ≥1,000 km². Global exposure is a separate global aggregation. Statistics use the verified corrected lookup and cell-area calculation, retaining zero-inundation years. River reaches with VIC benchmark mean runoff below 18.25 mm yr⁻¹ are excluded; inundated area uses depth >0.15 m. The 2000 VIC map is a separate hindcast, reprocessed with corrected lookup coordinates on 29 October 2026. Maps retain physical water depths; the dry-region screen applies to analysis aggregates.
